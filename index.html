02 / The long-range population debate

100 million Canadians
by 2100?

The Century Initiative advocates long-term population growth toward 100 million by 2100. It argues that a larger population can strengthen economic competitiveness and global influence. That thesis deserves scrutiny: total GDP is not the same as GDP per person, and growth requires housing, infrastructure and productive investment.

The organization's argument

Scale, labour and influence.

Century Initiative says an aging population, labour needs and geopolitical competition make coordinated long-term growth important. It explicitly rejects the characterization that it advocates uncontrolled migration.

Read its published position ↗
Questions for independent analysis

Who gains from scale?

How would a much larger population affect real wages, housing costs, productivity, water and energy demand, infrastructure, and national identity? What would slower-growth alternatives look like?

03 / Policy under examination

Canada first.
Capacity before commitments.

A policy agenda centred on lower immigration admissions, enforceable removal decisions, fiscal accountability and Canadian domestic priorities.

01 / Admissions

Reduce immigration admissions

Compare lower permanent and temporary admission targets against housing construction, infrastructure capacity, workforce demand and household incomes.

02 / Enforcement

Enforce removal orders

Track outstanding removal orders, appeals, backlogs, voluntary departures, enforcement costs and completion rates.

03 / Protection

Asylum policy

Audit asylum intake, adjudication delays, fiscal costs and alternative statutory frameworks.

04 / Affordability

Housing before targets

Measure construction completions, vacancies, land-use constraints, mortgage costs and real household incomes alongside population growth.

05 / Sovereignty

Domestic prosperity

Assess productivity, Canadian ownership, strategic industries, resource development, energy security and resilience to foreign economic pressure.

06 / Heritage

Preserve Canadian heritage

Document Canada's historical institutions, Indigenous, French and British foundations, constitutional traditions, architecture, languages and local heritage.

04 / Evidence standard

Every claim.
Traceable.

Every quantitative claim includes its date, population, denominator, methodology and primary source. Correlations, causal estimates and policy scenarios are labelled separately.
